Approach

Built around clarity, systems thinking, and restraint.

The most interesting part of software is where product decisions and engineering decisions start affecting each other.

Most academic software solves the function and forgets the experience. My work sits in that gap: translating cluttered student workflows into interfaces that are easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to come back to.

At ITER, the classroom sharpens the fundamentals. Outside it, hands-on product building forces better decisions around structure, state, data, responsiveness, and maintainability. That combination is what makes these projects valuable to me.

What I care about most is building something coherent, where the visual layer, interaction design, and backend logic all feel like parts of the same system instead of disconnected pieces.

01

Clarity over noise

Reduce friction, tighten hierarchy, and keep the interface useful under real student workloads.

02

Systems over one-offs

Reusable patterns, cleaner structure, and decisions that scale beyond a single screen or feature.

03

Polish with purpose

Motion, surfaces, and copy should reinforce trust, not compensate for weak product thinking.
